Cosmic Scale — Privacy Policy

Effective 18 November 2026

Cosmic Scale ("the app") is published by Khushnidjon Keldiboev. This policy covers the iPhone app and explains what information it handles.

What the app itself collects

Nothing about you. The app has no accounts and no sign-in, and its only server is the small one behind the space chat, described below. What it stores stays on your device: the language you chose, when your Beyond pack and Ultra unlocks expire, which simulations you have unlocked, and whether Cosmic Scale Pro is on. It never leaves your iPhone. The only things the app sends anywhere are the crash reports, the usage statistics and the notifications described next, the questions you ask in the space chat, and what the advertising, purchase, photo and map services below receive.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, it can send a report to Google's Firebase Crashlytics so the fault can be found and fixed. The report holds technical details of the failure (which part of the app's code was running), the app version, your iPhone's model and iOS version, its free memory and storage, and a random installation identifier that Firebase creates. It holds no name, email address or location, nothing you typed, and it is not used for advertising. If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, reports are sent only if you agree in the consent form that appears when the app starts. Test builds send none. Firebase processes them for us under Google's terms: see Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Usage statistics

To see which parts of the app people use, the app can send anonymous usage statistics to Google Analytics for Firebase. It records events such as which screens and simulations are opened, how far the welcome tour gets, whether a video was watched to unlock something, whether an ad was tapped, whether Pro was bought, and which language is set. It sends no name, email address, location, advertising identifier or anything you typed: the app includes Google Analytics without its advertising-identifier support, and ad personalisation is turned off for it. Google Analytics adds the technical details every app report has: the app version, your iPhone's model, iOS version and language, your country and city (worked out from your IP address, which Google does not keep), and a random installation identifier. Google processes this for us under its terms: see Privacy and Security in Firebase.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, nothing is collected unless you agree in the consent form that appears when the app starts. Everywhere, you can turn it off in Settings → Share usage statistics, which also deletes what Google Analytics holds for your installation. The statistics are not collected by test builds.

The same kind of usage events also go to PostHog, a product analytics service, which keeps them for us in its US cloud and lets us adjust a few of the app's settings, such as how often ads appear, without a new version. They record which screens and features are used, purchases and ads, with the app version, your iPhone's model, iOS version and language, and a random identifier made for this installation. Like any web request, PostHog receives your iPhone's IP address, from which it can work out a rough region. No name, email address, advertising identifier or anything you typed is sent, and session replay (recording the screen) is off. If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, nothing goes to PostHog unless you agree in the same consent form, and everywhere Settings → Share usage statistics turns it off too. See PostHog's Privacy Policy.

Notifications

"Today in space", the Sky alerts and the reminders after a few days away are set on your iPhone and need no account or server. For news and tips, the app uses Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple's push notification service: the app joins a notification topic for its language, which makes Firebase create a random installation identifier and a message token for your iPhone. Nothing else about you is sent, and the topic holds no personal information. You can turn news and tips off in the app's Settings (the app then leaves the topic), or turn notifications off in your iPhone's Settings. Firebase processes this for us under Google's terms: see Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Advertising

The app is free and shows ads through Google AdMob, unless Cosmic Scale Pro is on: then the ad SDK is not started at all. The app does not track you across other companies' apps and websites, and it never asks for App Tracking Transparency permission: it does not use your device's advertising identifier for tracking. To serve and measure ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK may collect and process information such as your IP address (from which approximate location can be inferred), device and app information, and interactions with ads. Google's use of this information is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

On an iPhone in Russia, where Google serves no ads, the app shows ads from the Yandex Advertising Network instead, through the Yandex Mobile Ads SDK (which includes Yandex's AppMetrica component). To serve and measure ads, that SDK may collect and process information such as your IP address (from which approximate location can be inferred), device and app information, and interactions with ads. The app turns off its location tracking, so it does not collect your location. Yandex's use of this information is described in the Yandex Privacy Policy. As with Google's ads, the app never asks for App Tracking Transparency permission. The app starts Yandex's SDK only on an iPhone it recognises as being in Russia (by the App Store country or the time zone), and never when Cosmic Scale Pro is on. If such an iPhone is in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, Yandex's ads there are not personalised.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, the app asks for your consent through Google's User Messaging Platform before personalised ads are shown. You can change your choice at any time from About → Privacy choices in the app.

You can also limit ad personalisation in Settings → Privacy & Security → Apple Advertising on your iPhone.

Space chat

The space chat (the ✨ button) answers questions about space. When you send a question, the app sends it, together with up to three earlier questions and answers from the same conversation, the app's language and a random identifier made only for the chat, to our small service on Cloudflare Workers. That service passes the questions and answers to Anthropic's Claude, which writes the reply. Like any web request, Cloudflare receives your iPhone's IP address.

The service keeps no copy of your questions or the answers. It keeps only a count of questions per day for the chat identifier and for your IP address, so that nobody can go over the daily limit; the counts start again each day. Anthropic processes the questions and answers to write the reply, under its commercial terms, which do not allow it to train its models on them: see Anthropic's Privacy Policy. The app does not store your conversation, and nothing is sent until you send a question. Please don't type personal information into the chat.

If the service can't be reached, an iPhone with Apple Intelligence may answer on the iPhone itself instead; that answer is worked out on your device and nothing is sent.

Purchases

Cosmic Scale Pro is sold through the App Store, as a monthly or yearly subscription or a one-time purchase. Apple handles the payment; the app never sees your payment details. It only asks the App Store, through Apple's StoreKit, which Pro purchases your Apple Account owns, to turn Pro on, and keeps that answer on your iPhone. Apple's handling of purchases is described in the Apple Privacy Policy. You can manage or cancel a subscription in your App Store account settings at any time.

Photos from space agencies

When you open an object's details, the app loads real photographs directly from the servers of NASA (images-assets.nasa.gov), ESA/Hubble (esahubble.org), ESA/Webb (esawebb.org), ESO (eso.org) and NSF NOIRLab (noirlab.edu). Like any website, those servers receive your device's IP address and a standard request for the image. The app sends them nothing else. Their own privacy policies apply to those requests.

Sounds from space missions

When you tap Listen, the app streams that sound or sonification from NASA (nasa.gov), the Chandra X-ray Center (chandra.harvard.edu) or the University of Iowa (uiowa.edu). As with photos, those servers receive your IP address and the request, nothing else. Nothing is streamed until you tap Listen. The tour music is generated on your device.

Satellite imagery of Earth

When you zoom in on Earth far enough, the app shows Apple Maps satellite imagery through Apple's MapKit. Apple's servers receive your device's IP address and a request for the imagery of the area on screen, like any use of Apple Maps. This request does not use your location; Apple is sent nothing else, and nothing is requested until you zoom in that far. Apple's handling of these requests is described in the Apple Privacy Policy.

Sky can use your location, only if you tap Use my location: it is rounded to about 10 km, stays on your phone and is used only to work out which eclipses you can see from there and, for Meteor Night, to find dark places near you on a map of night-sky brightness that is built into the app. It is never sent anywhere. If you tap Directions to one of those places, the app opens the maps app you pick (such as Apple Maps or Google Maps) with that place as the destination; that app works out the route under its own privacy policy.

Meteor Night can show your camera's picture behind the meteor guides, so they sit on the real sky, only if you allow camera access. The picture is shown live on your screen and nothing else: it is not recorded, stored or sent.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them.
