How to make a video smaller than 2 GB

To get a video under Telegram's 2 GB limit, convert it to a lower bitrate, which usually means a lower quality setting or a lower resolution, or make it shorter. A video's file size is roughly its bitrate multiplied by its length, so those are the only two things you can change.

Why the size is what it is

Bitrate is how much data the video uses per second, usually given in megabits per second (Mbit/s). The arithmetic is simple:

size in bytes ≈ (video bitrate + audio bitrate) × length in seconds ÷ 8

The real ceiling on a free Telegram account is 2,097,152,000 bytes, which is about 16,777 megabits. Divide that by the length of the video and you get the highest average bitrate that can fit:

  • a 30-minute video (1,800 seconds) can average up to just over 9.3 Mbit/s;
  • a 60-minute video (3,600 seconds) can average up to just over 4.6 Mbit/s;
  • a 2-hour video (7,200 seconds) can average up to just over 2.3 Mbit/s.

These are the limits for video and sound together, so aim a little below them. The file also holds some extra data besides the picture and sound, and a file that misses by a few megabytes still will not send.

Steps

  1. Check the exact size of the video in bytes. If it is over 2,097,152,000 bytes, it needs to shrink.
  2. Cut anything you do not need from the start and the end. A shorter video is smaller at the same quality.
  3. Convert the video at a lower bitrate. Pick a target a little under the limit worked out above.
  4. If the result still looks poor, lower the resolution as well, for example from 4K to 1080p. Fewer pixels need less data for the same sharpness.
  5. Check the new size in bytes, then send the new file. Keep the original until you have checked the copy.

Telegram's own Quality setting

When you send a video the normal way in Telegram's iPhone app, it shows a Quality setting with steps from Very Low to Very High and converts the video before sending. A lower step gives a smaller file. Telegram does not say how large each step will be, so for a long video close to the limit, check the size yourself or convert it before you send.

If you want to keep the full quality instead, you can send the video as a file, but then the original has to fit under 2 GB on its own.

Splitting into parts

You can also cut a long video into several parts, each under 2 GB. It keeps the quality, but the video is now several files that have to be watched in order and kept together. Converting to one smaller file is usually easier to live with.

Telegram Premium

With Telegram Premium the per-file limit is 4 GB, according to Telegram's FAQ. That doubles the room, but the same arithmetic applies, and anyone on an older Telegram app may not be able to open files over 2 GB, as Telegram's terms note.
