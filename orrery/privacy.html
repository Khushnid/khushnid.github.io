Orrery — Privacy Policy

Effective 23 October 2026

Orrery shows the solar system as it really is, drawn on your iPhone. It is built to need as little about you as possible.

What Orrery collects

Orrery itself collects almost nothing: it has no accounts and no analytics of its own, and it sends nothing to a server of its own. It does not track you across other companies' apps and websites, and it does not sell or share personal information. It does not use your location, contacts, photos or microphone. The free plan shows ads, served by Google; what that involves is described under Advertising below.

What stays on your device

Your settings — language, time speed, graphics quality and the like — your weight and its unit if you type them in to see what you would weigh on other worlds (they are used only to do that sum on your iPhone), the day you were born if you enter it for Your birthday sky (it is used only to work out your age on other worlds and where the planets stood that day, on your iPhone), whether Orrery Pro is unlocked, what a video has opened for half an hour, and the dates and counts that keep ads from appearing too often are kept on your iPhone only, so the app opens the way you left it. Deleting the app deletes them.

Ask Orrery, where your iPhone offers it, is answered by the language model built into iOS (Apple Intelligence), which runs on your iPhone. What you ask, and what it answers, is handled there: it is not sent to Orrery, to Google or to any server of ours, and Orrery does not keep it once you close the conversation. When you ask it to fly somewhere, change the date or weigh you, it does that on your iPhone too, using the weight you saved on the device.

Sky alerts, if you turn them on, are notifications that Orrery schedules on your iPhone itself, for eclipses, planets at their best, meteor showers and the like. The dates are worked out on your iPhone from the positions of the planets that Orrery already carries; nothing about you or about the alerts is sent to Orrery or to anyone else, Orrery uses no push-notification service, and you choose whether to allow them, in the sheet that offers them or in Settings, and can turn them off or take the permission back at any time in iOS Settings.

Dark Side, the ship game

Dark Side is a game of ships in the sky. In this version it is practice against bots, played entirely on your iPhone. The game does not connect to any server, and it sends nothing about you or about how you play. There is no account, no chat and no free text, so nobody can send you a message. On the free plan an ad from Google can follow a practice flight, as described under Advertising below, but never during one.

Your callsign (a star's name and a number; you cannot type your own), your level and experience, your flight and win counts, your streak, your daily missions, your ship colour and your settings are kept on your iPhone only, so the game remembers you. Deleting the app deletes them.

What the app downloads

To show photographs, recordings and surface maps, Orrery downloads public files from NASA and the U.S. Geological Survey (for example images.nasa.gov and trek.nasa.gov). These requests carry nothing about you beyond what any web request does — your device's IP address, seen by the server that answers. Orrery keeps the files it downloads on your device so it need not fetch them again.

Advertising

The free plan shows two kinds of ads from Google AdMob. A full-screen ad can appear, but only at a natural pause, right after you finish or close something: a mission tour, a facts sheet, a look inside a world, Earth's magnetic field or the galaxy, the sky events sheet, your birthday sky, a visit to a world's surface, or a Dark Side practice flight (no ad follows your first three flights). Not every pause brings one. None appears the very first time Orrery is opened, in the first minutes after you install it, in the first couple of minutes after Orrery starts up or after you come back from a long break, or soon after another ad or a video you chose to watch. None appears during the 30 minutes after a video has opened something for you, in the introduction, on the Orrery Pro screen, or while you are in the middle of something, such as a flight or a gesture.

The other kind is a short video you may choose to watch. If you tap something locked and close the Orrery Pro screen without buying, Orrery can offer it, and if you watch it to the end it opens that one thing (a mission, a world's inside or surface, a sound, a view, the sharpest picture or a ship colour) for 30 minutes. Orrery Pro removes all ads.

To show and measure ads, Google's software on your iPhone may collect your device's identifiers, your approximate location (derived from your IP address), how you interact with ads, and diagnostic information about the app, and Google may combine these as described in its own policies: policies.google.com/technologies/ads. What the software collects is listed at developers.google.com/admob/ios/privacy/data-disclosure. So that an ad is ready when a pause comes, Orrery asks Google for ads in the background, starting a little after it opens (after your consent, where it is required); an ad that is asked for is not always shown. Orrery does not ask for permission to track you across other apps and websites, so ads are served without your device's advertising identifier. What you are looking at in Orrery is never sent to Google.

Where the law requires it (the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and some other regions), Orrery asks for your consent in Google's consent form before any ad is requested. The form comes up at one of the pauses above or when you choose to watch a video, never as Orrery opens. Where that applies, you can change your choice at any time in Settings, under Privacy choices (it is hidden with Orrery Pro, which has no ads).

Purchases

Orrery Pro is bought through Apple's App Store. Apple handles the payment; Orrery never sees your payment details or your Apple Account. The app asks the App Store only whether Pro is unlocked. Apple's own privacy policy covers the purchase: apple.com/legal/privacy.

Sharing

Sharing a still uses the system share sheet, and the image goes only where you send it.

Children

Orrery is made for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13. Orrery itself sends nothing about anyone to a server of its own, and Dark Side, which is played against bots on the iPhone, does not go online; the ads on the free plan are served by Google as described above.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will appear here and in the app, with its date.
